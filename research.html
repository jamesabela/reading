The Research Behind Concept Grids

Concept grids are supported by a strong foundation of educational and cognitive science research. While the term "concept grid" is modern, the underlying principles are well established and widely used in classrooms.

1. Frayer Model

  • ✔️ Developed as a structured approach to teaching vocabulary
  • ✔️ Includes definition, characteristics, examples, and non-examples
  • ✔️ Helps learners develop deeper understanding

2. Graphic Organisers

  • ✔️ Visual structures that organise information clearly
  • ✔️ Improve comprehension and retention
  • ✔️ Help students connect new ideas to prior knowledge

3. Dual Coding Theory

  • ✔️ Learning improves when information is visual and verbal
  • ✔️ Concept grids combine structured text with visual organisation

4. Depth of Processing

  • ✔️ Deeper thinking leads to better memory
  • ✔️ Analysing examples is more effective than simple definitions

5. Vocabulary Instruction

  • ✔️ Multiple exposures are needed to new vocabulary
  • ✔️ Words should be explored in different contexts

6. Retrieval Practice

  • ✔️ Actively recalling information strengthens memory
  • ✔️ Filling in grids supports active recall

Summary

Concept grids bring together research-backed strategies including the Frayer Model, graphic organisers, dual coding, depth of processing, and retrieval practice. By encouraging learners to explore vocabulary through multiple perspectives, concept grids improve understanding, retention, and the ability to use new words effectively.
